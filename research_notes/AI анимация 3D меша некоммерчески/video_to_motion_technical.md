## Q1. Current best monocular 3D human mesh/pose recovery models and their trade-offs

### Takeaway
There is now a single consistent apples-to-apples table (from OnlineHMR, arXiv 2603.17355) covering WHAM / TRAM / GVHMR / PromptHMR / Human3R on both camera-frame and world-frame metrics: **PromptHMR is the accuracy leader on both** (EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE 40.1 mm, EMDB-2 W-MPJPE₁₀₀ 216.5 mm, RTE 1.3 %), **TRAM is a very close second on world-frame but is the slowest at 2.1 FPS**, and **GVHMR is the best speed/accuracy compromise at 12.1 FPS** — which is why every ComfyUI node pack wraps GVHMR and not the others. The 2026 arrival that changes the practical picture is **SAM 3D Body**, which is now in ComfyUI *core* with GLB and BVH export, removing the install problem entirely for single-image/tracked-video body+hands.

- **PA-MPJPE vs W-MPJPE, why it matters:** EMDB-2 is the world-coordinate split; papers cut sequences into 100-frame segments and report **W-MPJPE** (aligns only the first two frames) alongside **WA-MPJPE** (aligns the whole segment). PA-MPJPE is Procrustes-aligned, i.e. rotation/translation/scale removed — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17355)

### Inferences
- For animation, the metric ordering should be: **RTE and W-MPJPE₁₀₀ first** (they govern whether the character slides, drifts or scales wrong across a shot), **Accel/jitter second** (governs how much smoothing you must apply, and smoothing is what kills fast limbs), and **PA-MPJPE last** — PA-MPJPE discards global rotation, translation *and scale*, which is precisely the information a retarget needs. A model can win PA-MPJPE and still be unusable: WHAM's 38.2 PA-MPJPE is the best in the table while its RTE of 6.0 % is 4–5× worse than TRAM/PromptHMR.
- The ~216 mm best-case W-MPJPE₁₀₀ means that over a 100-frame (≈3–4 s) window, even the best model is ~20 cm off in world space. For a 1.75 m character that is >10 % of body height. Practical consequence: **do not trust world-frame root translation over long shots**; cut at 2–4 s, or keep the root locked and re-author translation by hand.
- GVHMR being the only model wrapped by ComfyUI node packs is explained by the speed table (12.1 FPS, 2× WHAM, 6× TRAM) plus the fact that it needs no SLAM when the camera is static (`-s`) — and for generated driving video the camera *is* static by construction, so the `-s` path is the correct one and DPVO/SimpleVO never has to be built. **This removes the single worst install dependency in the whole field.**
- SAM 3D Body in ComfyUI core is now the lowest-friction path to a skeleton: no conda env, no detectron2, no SMPL registration wall in the node (though the HF weights are gated), and BVH out of the box. Its weakness is that it is fundamentally a **single-image** model with temporal smoothing bolted on (`SAM3DBody_Smooth`), not a world-frame video model — so expect good per-frame pose and poor global trajectory.
- MHR separating skeleton from surface shape is architecturally the right thing for retargeting (you want joint angles, not a blendshape soup), and is a reason to prefer SAM 3D Body output over raw SMPL betas if your target rig is not SMPL.

### Gaps
- **VRAM figures for GVHMR, TRAM, WHAM, PromptHMR, CameraHMR, SMPLer-X, 4D-Humans are not published** on their repo landing pages or in the papers I could reach. The only hard numbers in the whole field are Human3R's self-reported 8 GB and ActionMesh's 32 GB / 12 GB `--low_ram`. Everything else must be measured locally.
- The main GVHMR install does not state a torch version. The only pinned combination anywhere in its docs is torch 2.3.0 + CUDA 12.1 (for the optional DPVO wheel), so that is the safest guess but it is a guess.
- No paper I found reports **hand support** explicitly for GVHMR/TRAM/WHAM. GVHMR requires SMPL-X files for prediction and SMPL for rendering/eval, which implies an SMPL-X-shaped output, but whether its hand pose is actually predicted (vs left at mean pose) is unconfirmed.
- **No PA-MPJPE is reported for GVHMR, PromptHMR or Human3R in the EMDB-2 world table** — the authors left those cells blank.
- **No independent EMDB leaderboard exists.** Papers-with-Code-style aggregators (wizwand) list EMDB rows with inconsistent metric labels; I could not date or source individual rows and would not rely on them — [Source](https://www.wizwand.com/dataset/emdb)
- SMPLer-X's official code/weights location and its install difficulty were not confirmed.
- No 2026 release was found that is a clear successor to GVHMR for single-person world-frame work. PromptHMR (CVPR 2025) remains the accuracy leader in the only consistent table available.

## Q2. The fast-limb problem: why models under-shoot fast limb extension, and documented fixes

### Takeaway
**This is the weakest-evidenced part of the whole assignment.** I found no paper that measures limb-extension under-shoot as such, and no paper that tests frame interpolation as a pose-estimation pre-process. What does exist and is directly relevant: a body of work on pose from *motion blur* (Human from Blur, ICCV 2023; a 3DV 2026 paper on ultra-fast blur but for rigid objects only), documented evidence that 2D-reprojection-only refitting **worsens** depth error rather than fixing it (KITRO), and diffusion motion priors (RoHM) as the credible cleanup route.

### Cited Findings
- **Motion blur is the named mechanism.** "A particular difficulty occurs when the human motion is fast, or low light conditions demand longer camera exposure times, which can both lead to blurry images." Human from Blur (ICCV 2023; Yiming Zhao, Denys Rozumnyi, Jie Song, Otmar Hilliges, Marc Pollefeys, Martin R. Oswald) treats pose estimation as the **inverse of deblurring**: a 3D human model + texture map + a sequence of poses describes the motion, a temporal aggregation step models the blur, and a differentiable renderer backpropagates pixel-wise reprojection error to recover **sub-frame human motion and shape**. The authors add regularisation terms because reconstruction loss alone is insufficient, and report consistently outperforming other methods on significantly blurry inputs — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17209)
- Same paper states there was **no public dataset** for this task "since none of standard datasets for human pose estimation include significant amounts of motion blur," and that it evaluates on 21 real-world sequences with significant motion blur, using synthetic high-speed data plus a real dataset **built with frame interpolation** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17209v3)
- **2D-reprojection-only refitting is documented to make depth worse.** KITRO: "optimizing solely on 2D reprojection does not account for depth ambiguity so is unlikely to find the optimal solution and may even increase the depth error." KITRO's own fix is refining the mesh using 2D clues plus **kinematic-tree rotation** rather than free optimisation — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.19833)
- **The shape/limb-length ambiguity in SMPLify is a known structural weakness:** original SMPLify is "relatively well-aligned in 3D pose, but the shape remains highly unconstrained since the connection length between two keypoints is the only indicator that can be used to estimate the body shape" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.01923v6)
- **SMPLify-style refitting actively hallucinates when keypoints disagree with the body prior.** ResiHMR (CVPR 2026): SMPLify-X and HSMR "hallucinate intact limbs or distort the lower body due to their intact-limb priors and fixed kinematic topologies"; when directly fitted to residual-limb keypoints it "tends to hallucinate missing distal segments and place residual endpoints at the nearest intact joint, leading to anatomically incorrect meshes and **unstable optimization near the limb termination**." Reported gains: residual-limb 2D MPJPE 73.61 → 23.19 with HSMR, intact-joint 2D MPJPE 41.32 → 37.40 with SMPLify-X — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.28025); [CVPR PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Ying_ResiHMR_Residual-Limb_Aware_Single-Image_3D_Human_Mesh_Recovery_for_Individuals_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- **Deblurring as a pre-process gives a measurable but modest gain.** A Scientific Reports (Sept 2026) sports-training framework by Li et al. pairs motion deblurring and tracking with a lightweight HRNet-style estimator (GSANet); the deblurring step "gave a modest but statistically supported improvement in PCKh on a small sports dataset," while GSANet itself cuts parameters/compute versus HRNet-W32 at lower absolute accuracy — [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-72761-y)
- **Video pose methods still degrade specifically on fast motion.** A Pattern Recognition (March 2026) paper states existing video methods "still suffer from severe performance degeneration especially in challenging cases," naming blur in fast motion scenes — [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0031320325008544)
- **The 2026 ultra-fast-blur paper is not about humans.** "Recovering 3D Shapes from Ultra-Fast Motion-Blurred Images" (arXiv 2602.07860v1, 8 Feb 2026; Fei Yu, Shudan Guo, Shiqing Xin, Beibei Wang, Haisen Zhao, Wenzheng Chen; accepted 3DV 2026) is fully differentiable inverse rendering through a blur simulator, with a faster barycentric-weight solver giving up to **4.57× speedup**. Its examples are balls and rotating machinery; **humans are not mentioned**, and code release is not confirmed — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07860)
- **Event cameras are the other documented route.** Event-based pose work notes that "motion blur cripples traditional frame-based camera systems during high-speed human motion," with DHP19 as the event-camera dataset with mocap 3D ground truth — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11910); [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.07034)
- A survey-level view notes complementary sensors (depth, IMUs) "help to improve pose predictions when cameras struggle with motion blur" — [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11430-4)
- **The credible cleanup tool is a diffusion motion prior, not an optimiser.** RoHM (CVPR 2024 oral, Zhang et al., ETH Zürich + Meta Reality Labs) takes noisy, partly-occluded 3D motion from monocular video and produces complete motion in **consistent global coordinates**. It explicitly **separates a global root-trajectory model from a local body-pose model**, linked by a conditioning module with an iterative inference scheme refining both; covers reconstruction, denoising, and **spatial and temporal infilling**; predicts foot-ground contact for physical plausibility; authors report better quality than prior methods on three datasets with faster test-time runs. Project page `sanweiliti.github.io/ROHM` — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08570v2); [CVPR PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Zhang_RoHM_Robust_Human_Motion_Reconstruction_via_Diffusion_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)
- **GVHMR's test-time post-processing is what produces its published global metrics.** The repo states "training skips the test-time post-processing, so global metrics will differ from the reported test numbers" — i.e. a retrained GVHMR without post-processing is measurably worse globally than the paper claims — [Source](https://github.com/zju3dv/GVHMR)
- **Frame interpolation as a general pre-process is an established idea in the ComfyUI world but untested for pose:** ComfyUI's own docs say interpolation "can also be used as a preprocessing step, allowing you to standardize frame rate early and feed cleaner temporal data into larger animation and video pipelines" — a general tip, **not a pose benchmark** — [Source](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/frame-interpolation). RIFE itself uses an IFNet that estimates intermediate flows end-to-end, 4–27× faster than SuperSlomo and DAIN — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06294)

### Inferences
- **The causal chain, as supported by the sources:** fast limb → motion blur within the exposure → the 2D keypoint detector's heatmap peak lands at the blur centroid (the time-average of the limb's sweep) rather than at either extreme → the lifted 3D pose is the time-average → the extension looks under-shot. Human from Blur's framing of the problem as the *inverse of deblurring* and its recovery of *sub-frame* motion is direct evidence that the information is in the blur and is being thrown away by a single-peak detector. Note this is my synthesis; no source states it in exactly these terms.
- **The second, compounding cause is temporal smoothing.** Every world-frame model reports Accel/jitter as a metric it is optimising down (WHAM 5.3–6.6, TRAM 4.9), and GVHMR's global numbers depend on undisclosed test-time post-processing. A smoothness objective is mathematically a low-pass filter, and the peak of a fast limb extension is the highest-frequency content in the signal. **Conclusion: the fast-limb defect is partly a feature, deliberately traded for the jitter metric.** Practical implication: find and turn down the post-processing/smoothing strength before trying anything else.
- **Naive SMPLify-style 2D keypoint refitting is the wrong fix and will probably make things worse,** on two independently sourced grounds: KITRO shows 2D-reprojection-only optimisation can *increase* depth error, and ResiHMR shows the fitting becomes unstable exactly at limb terminations when keypoints disagree with the body prior. If you refit, use a kinematic-tree-constrained formulation (KITRO's approach) rather than free parameter optimisation, and do not let the optimiser touch betas.
- **Higher frame rate input is the only fix that attacks the root cause**, because shorter exposure = less blur per frame. But that means **filming at a high shutter speed**, not interpolating afterwards: RIFE-style interpolation synthesises intermediate frames from the already-blurred ones and cannot recover sub-frame extremes that were averaged away. Interpolated frames are plausible-looking inventions; feeding them to a pose model is at best neutral and may inject artefacts. **No source tests this either way** — treat interpolation-as-mocap-upsampling as unverified and likely useless for recovering peak extension. Its one defensible use is after extraction, to resample a 24 fps motion curve to 30/60 fps for your target engine.
- **For generated driving video this problem is largely sidestepped, but in a dishonest way:** video diffusion models render fast limbs with synthetic blur or with implausibly crisp limbs, and either way the "ground truth" motion never existed. You get a clean extraction of a motion that is not physically correct. This is worth stating plainly in any operator's manual.
- RoHM's architecture (separate global-trajectory and local-pose diffusion models, with temporal infilling) is the closest published thing to a general-purpose motion cleaner, and its infilling capability is in principle the right tool for *re-creating* a peak extension that smoothing destroyed — mask the frames around the peak and let the prior infill. I found no source that does this, so it is an untested idea.

### Gaps

## Q3. Multi-person and contact

### Takeaway
The 2026 state of the art is **Multi-HMR 2** (NAVER LABS Europe), which gets identity consistency by distilling SAM2 memory features rather than by bolting a 2D tracker on — architecturally the right answer to the ID-swap-on-contact problem. For a practical local pipeline, TRAM natively handles multi-person while GVHMR does not, and SAM 3.1 video tracks now feed multi-person detection in ComfyUI core.

### Cited Findings
- **Multi-HMR 2** (arXiv 2606.14841, June 2026, NAVER LABS Europe): DETR-based, does detection + mesh recovery + tracking in one model; "distills image-based memory features from SAM2, extending to tracking and achieving **consistent identity association without video supervision**"; claims improvement over SOTA in interaction recovery and scene placement; reports results on **close interactions in CMU Panoptic** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14841)
- **Multi-THuMBS** (arXiv 2607.01626, July 2026): tracks multiple 3D human meshes **across shot changes**, noting prior 3D mesh tracking under shot changes "is still limited to single-person scenarios"; registers meshes in a shared 3D space maintaining per-person identity and motion consistency across cuts — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01626)
- **Original Multi-HMR** (NAVER LABS Europe, ECCV 2024): single-shot multi-person whole-body recovery predicting "the whole body, including hands and facial expressions, using the SMPL-X parametric model and 3D location in the camera coordinate system" — [Source](https://physics.paperswithcode.com/paper/multi-hmr-multi-person-whole-body-human-mesh)
- **A hard ID-switch number, from a non-video approach:** PressTrack-HMR (arXiv 2511.09147, Nov 2025) uses pressure-sensing floor footprint tracking instead of video appearance, targeting complex multi-person interaction; on its dataset it logged **437 ID switches across 86 trajectories, ≈1 switch per 523 frames** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09147)
- **TRAM is multi-person, GVHMR is not:** per Human3R's comparison, "GVHMR ... is limited to estimating a single person"; TRAM "natively models multi-person human mesh recovery" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06219). TRAM's pipeline includes Segment-Anything and **DEVA-Track-Anything**, and its step 1 "also detect+track humans" — [Source](https://github.com/yufu-wang/tram)
- **PromptHMR's prompt interface is the practical anti-swap tool:** it accepts spatial prompts (boxes, masks) and semantic **interaction labels**, estimates people from boxes as small as faces in crowded scenes, and explicitly "models person-person interactions" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06397v2)
- **In ComfyUI core**, `SAM3_VideoTrack` supplies "SAM 3.1 video tracks for multiperson detection" to the SAM 3D Body nodes — [Source](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-23-sam-3d-body-native-comfyui); the standalone `ComfyUI-SAM3` pack (PozzettiAndrea) does SAM 3 image/video segmentation with text prompts, boxes and points, including **video propagation nodes** — [Source](https://github.com/PozzettiAndrea/ComfyUI-SAM3)

### Inferences
- The field has moved away from "detector + ByteTrack/BoT-SORT + re-ID" and toward **segmentation-memory-based identity** (SAM2/SAM3 memory features, used by both Multi-HMR 2 and the ComfyUI SAM 3.1 track path). This is the correct direction for contact: appearance re-ID fails precisely when two bodies overlap and share pixels, whereas a propagated segmentation memory carries identity through the occlusion.
- **For a two-body interacting shot on local hardware the pragmatic stack is: SAM 3 / SAM 3.1 video segmentation to get two stable per-person mask tracks → feed each mask track as a prompt into PromptHMR (which takes masks natively) or crop-and-run GVHMR per track.** Prompting with a mask you have verified by eye removes the tracker from the accuracy path entirely — you are no longer trusting an automatic association, you are supplying it.
- Multi-THuMBS matters for generated driving video only if you stitch multiple generated shots; a single locked-camera generated clip has no shot changes.

### Gaps
- **No paper reports ID-switch counts for video-based multi-person HMR on a close-contact benchmark** (Hi4D, CMU Panoptic). The only hard ID-switch figure I found is from the pressure-sensor paper, which is not a video method and so is not a usable baseline.
- **ByteTrack / BoT-SORT + re-ID specifically:** I found no 2025–2026 source benchmarking these as the tracking front-end for HMR on contact scenes. Their absence from the recent literature is itself weak evidence that the field considers them superseded, but I cannot cite that conclusion.
- **PHALP** was not covered by any search result; its current status, maintenance and relative accuracy are unverified.
- Multi-HMR 2 code/weight release status was not confirmed.

## Q4. Hands and face from video, and bolting them onto a body-only HMR result

### Takeaway
**WiLoR beats HaMeR on FreiHAND** by an independently-reported margin (PA-MPJPE 5.5 vs 6.0 mm) and, unlike HaMeR, does hand localisation and reconstruction in one model so you do not need a separate hand detector. For face, **SMIRK edges EMOCA** on an independent geometric benchmark. The large 2026 change is that you may not need to bolt anything on: SAM 3D Body covers body + feet + hands in one model, and has a `SAM3DBody_FaceExpression` node in ComfyUI core.

### Cited Findings
- **WiLoR vs HaMeR on FreiHAND**, reported by a third party (GeoHand, 2026): WiLoR PA-MPJPE **5.5** / PA-MPVPE **5.1** / F@5 **0.825**; HaMeR PA-MPJPE **6.0** / PA-MPVPE **5.7** / F@5 **0.785**. GeoHand chose both as "highly representative state-of-the-art transformer-based monocular frameworks with robust open-source weights" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17354)
- **Architectural difference that matters operationally:** HaMeR uses a high-capacity ViT trained on mixed 2D and 3D hand datasets, "directly predicting MANO parameters from cropped hand images"; WiLoR "integrat[es] hand localization and 3D reconstruction, which HaMeR's crop-based input does not do," and "focus[es] more on complex inputs in real videos, such as incomplete hand regions, multiple hands in the frame, or hands mixed with complex backgrounds and manipulated objects" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16444)
- **Shared failure mode:** "in complex object manipulation scenarios, RGB-only methods like HaMeR and WiLoR often struggle to infer correct spatial relationships" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17354)
- **Fast-HaMeR** (arXiv 2603.16444, 2026) distils HaMeR into a smaller student; best ConvNeXt-L student reaches ~8.1 J_err on its own benchmark table (**not comparable** to the FreiHAND figures above) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16444)
- **Name clash to avoid:** "HAMR" in the 2019 paper *End-to-end Hand Mesh Recovery from a Monocular RGB Image* is an unrelated earlier method, not HaMeR — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.09305)
- **SMIRK vs EMOCA, design:** SMIRK (CVPR 2024) replaces differentiable rendering with a **neural rendering module**, which the authors argue yields more accurate gradients for expressive reconstruction; EMOCA builds on DECA and adds an **emotion-consistency loss**, so its focus is emotional expression — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04104v2)
- **SMIRK's own user study** beat DECA, EMOCAv2, Deep3D and FOCUS, with EMOCAv2 the closest competitor — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04104)
- **Independent geometric benchmark (RealDenseFace, on NeRSemble SVFR):** SMIRK beats EMOCA on both neutral and posed L1 error — posed **2.276 vs 2.636**, lower is better — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09238)
- **Independent qualitative counterpoint (FIELDS, on BP4D):** "EMOCA tends to produce a shortened chin profile and SMIRK/TEASER may exhibit distortions around the mouth region" — neither is clearly superior on lower-face geometry — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21245)
- **FLAME licensing:** FLAME 2023 Open is **CC-BY-4.0**; earlier FLAME versions are non-commercial research only — [Source](https://flame.is.tue.mpg.de/)
- **SAM 3D Body covers hands natively:** it "estimates the pose of the body, feet, and hands," and MHR separates skeleton from surface shape — [Source](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/sam-3d-body-robust-full-body-human-mesh-recovery/)
- **ComfyUI core includes a face-expression node for it:** `SAM3DBody_FaceExpression` — [Source](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-23-sam-3d-body-native-comfyui)
- **GVHMR requires SMPL-X** (`SMPLX_{GENDER}.npz`) for predictions and evaluation, and SMPL (`SMPL_{GENDER}.pkl`) for rendering and evaluation — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zju3dv/GVHMR/main/docs/INSTALL.md)
- **SMPLer-X and Multi-HMR both output whole-body SMPL-X** including hands and facial expression — [Source](https://paperswithcode.com/paper/smpler-x-scaling-up-expressive-human-pose-and); [Source](https://physics.paperswithcode.com/paper/multi-hmr-multi-person-whole-body-human-mesh)

### Inferences
- **Prefer WiLoR over HaMeR** for a bolt-on hand stage: it is more accurate on FreiHAND *and* it removes the hand-detection-plus-crop stage, which in a video pipeline is where left/right confusion and crop jitter come from. HaMeR's crop-based design means you must supply a stable hand box per frame, and a jittery box produces jittery MANO output even if each frame is individually good.
- **The bolt-on mechanism is a parameter graft, not a mesh merge:** SMPL-X holds body pose, hand pose (MANO-compatible) and jaw/expression in one parameter vector, so the correct operation is to keep the body model's global orient + body_pose + betas + transl, overwrite `left_hand_pose`/`right_hand_pose` from WiLoR and `jaw_pose`/`expression` from SMIRK, then re-pose one SMPL-X body. The wrist joint is the seam: WiLoR predicts hand pose in its own crop frame, so you must align its wrist orientation to the body's forearm orientation or the hands will point the wrong way. **No source I found documents a published recipe for this alignment** — see Gaps.
- SMIRK and EMOCA are both single-frame, so any face track needs per-frame inference plus your own temporal smoothing; expect to need a different smoothing strength for jaw (fast) than for expression blendshapes (slow).
- Given SAM 3D Body does body+feet+hands in one MHR model inside ComfyUI core, the bolt-on route is now a fallback rather than the default. The case for bolting WiLoR on is when hands are the subject of the shot and you need the extra ~0.5 mm PA-MPJPE and robustness to occluding objects.
- For generated driving video specifically, hands are the single worst-rendered body part in video diffusion output. Extracting hand pose from generated video is likely to produce garbage regardless of which hand model you use, because the source pixels are wrong. **Prioritise body motion from generated video and author hands by hand.**

### Gaps
- **HandOccNet** returned no results in my searches; its current status and relative accuracy are unverified.
- **MediaPipe face** did not appear in any 2025–2026 comparison I found; no sourced accuracy figures relative to SMIRK/EMOCA/DECA/SPECTRE.
- **SPECTRE and DECA** appear only as names in comparison lists (DECA as SMIRK's and EMOCA's predecessor); I have no current benchmark numbers for them.
- **No published recipe or tool for the wrist-seam alignment** when grafting MANO hand pose onto a body-only HMR result. This is the concrete step the assignment asked for and I could not source it.
- **No source compares SMIRK vs EMOCA on temporal stability on video** — all comparisons found are single-frame.
- WiLoR's and HaMeR's install requirements, VRAM and speed were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **The right local tool depends on whether you have a pose sequence.** If you do not, Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B at 720p/24 fps is the sweet spot: it is the only official variant with a 24 GB floor, it is Apache-2.0, and 24 fps/720p is adequate for HMR input. If you do have a pose sequence, **Wan 2.2 Animate (not Animate 2) is the one to use**, because it is the version that still takes an explicit DWPose control sequence — Animate 2 deliberately removed the motion extractor and consumes a driving *video* instead, which is useless to you if what you hold is a skeleton.
- **The A14B 80 GB official floor means the 14 B Wan models are off the table on consumer hardware via the official path.** Everything below that number depends on ComfyUI offloading or GGUF quantisation, which are community routes with reported fidelity loss. For retarget-friendly output, fidelity loss mostly costs you limb-boundary sharpness, which is exactly what the 2D keypoint detector needs — so quantising hard to fit the model is self-defeating for this use case.
- The 4090 VAE-decode OOM report is the characteristic failure of this whole class of work: VRAM peaks at decode, not during denoising. Plan to decode in tiles or at lower resolution, and treat "it got through all the steps" as no evidence it will finish.
- **Prompting for retarget-friendly output** — I found no sourced prompting guide for this. Derived from the constraints the sources do establish: the camera must be static because GVHMR's fast `-s` path and TRAM's `--static_camera` flag both exist precisely to skip SLAM, and skipping SLAM removes the largest single source of world-frame error; the subject must be fully in frame because every HMR model is crop-based and a cropped limb is an invented limb; clothing must be tight because SMPL/SMPL-X fit a naked body surface and loose fabric biases both betas and the 2D keypoints; background must be clean because segmentation (SAM 3 / RMBG) is in the path and a busy background creates mask flicker which becomes pose jitter. **All four of these are inferences from the extraction side's documented requirements, not quotes from a prompting guide.**
- Wan 2.2 Animate's "input dimensions should be multiples of 16" is a hard constraint worth designing the whole shot around — pick a canvas like 720×1280 or 832×480 and keep it fixed across generation and extraction so no resampling ever touches the frames the keypoint detector sees.

## Q6. The round trip: is there value in pose→video→pose, and can pose-controlled video act as a motion cleaner or amplifier?

### Takeaway
**I found no source that evaluates the round trip, and no source that proposes pose-controlled video generation as a motion cleaner or amplifier.** The one 2026 system that is explicitly bidirectional by design is NVIDIA's JCA (see Q7), which supports video→rig-motion, rig-motion→video and keyframe control in one model — but it does so by keeping a *separate 3D motion model* rather than by round-tripping through pixels, which is itself an argument against the round trip. The credible tool for cleaning and amplifying motion is a diffusion **motion** prior operating on motion directly (RoHM), not a video model.

### Cited Findings
- **JCA's architecture is an explicit rejection of pixel round-tripping.** Its abstract states that prior work "handles only human skeletons or **lifts 2D pose maps to 3D**," and that JCA instead "keeps 3D motion in a separate model that supports arbitrary skeletons, linked to a pretrained video model by a few cross-attention layers." The model "also learns to locate each joint in video frames, which allows camera recovery" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28632)
- JCA supports all three directions in one model: video-to-rig-motion, rig-motion-to-video, and keyframe-based control of the 3D motion; it also does scene-aware animation from an image and joint text→(video+motion) — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)
- **Wan Animate 2 removed the pose intermediate deliberately:** the driving video is "fed directly into the diffusion transformer rather than through a motion extractor" — [Source](https://blog.comfy.org/p/wan-animate-2-is-now-available-in)
- **RoHM is the published cleaner/amplifier**, operating in motion space: takes noisy, partly-occluded monocular motion → complete motion in consistent global coordinates; separate global-trajectory and local-pose diffusion models; supports **denoising and spatial and temporal infilling**; predicts foot-ground contact — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08570v2)
- JCA's training set is ~**200 K pairs** from human and robot video plus scenes edited with a generative video editor to cover many skeletons; its motion branch can additionally train on **motion-only data**, "which is easier to collect and augment than paired video" — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)

### Inferences
- **The round trip is lossy in a way that cannot be recovered, and the loss is concentrated exactly where you care.** Going pose→video, the generator must invent clothing, lighting and limb silhouettes; going video→pose, the keypoint detector reads the blur centroid of a fast limb (Q2). So a round trip through pixels passes the motion through two independent low-pass-like stages. It cannot add information that was not in the input pose, and will subtract the high-frequency content.
- **However, there is one defensible use of the round trip that the loss argument does not kill: plausibility laundering.** A pose-controlled video model is trained on real video, so its generator has an implicit prior over what human motion looks like *rendered*. If you feed it a broken pose sequence, it will render something plausible rather than something faithful, and re-extracting gives you a motion that lies inside the real-video manifold. That is "cleaning" in a loose sense. It is also uncontrolled — you have no handle on what it changed. **RoHM does the same job with an explicit prior, a global/local split and a contact term, which is strictly preferable.** No source compares the two, so this is reasoning, not a finding.
- **As an amplifier the round trip is worse than useless.** Amplifying a fast limb means increasing the peak extension, and a video model has no mechanism to extend beyond the pose conditioning it was given — the control signal *is* the skeleton. A hand-authored scale on the joint angles, or RoHM's temporal infilling over a masked peak, are both more direct.
- **The genuinely valuable asymmetry is JCA's, not the round trip's.** JCA's design says: keep motion in motion space, use the video model only as a supervision signal and as a renderer, and use joint localisation in frames to recover the camera. If the weights ever ship, that is the architecture to use for both directions. Until then, the practical analogue is: extract with a dedicated HMR model, clean in motion space, and render with Wan Animate only for *preview*, never as part of the extraction loop.
- One real operational use for pose→video that is not a round trip at all: **render your extracted motion through Wan 2.2 Animate as a visual diff against the original footage.** Side-by-side, a flattened limb or a drifting root is far more visible as rendered video than as a wireframe. That uses the generator as a review tool, where its invented detail is harmless.

### Gaps
- **No source evaluates pose→video→pose accuracy.** There is no published measurement of how much a round trip degrades (or changes) a motion sequence. The assignment asked whether there is value in the round trip and the honest answer is that nobody has measured it.
- **No source proposes or tests pose-controlled video models as a motion cleaner or amplifier.** The idea appears nowhere in the literature I could reach.
- Whether Wan 2.2 Animate accepts a synthesised (non-extracted) DWPose sequence is unverified — this blocks even trying the experiment.

**(a) NVIDIA JCA — CONFIRMED, no code**
- **Title:** "Joints, Camera, Action! Co-Generating 3D Motion and Video for Arbitrary Rigs"; **arXiv 2610.28632**, cs.CV, **v1 submitted Tue, 20 Oct 2026 17:59:56 UTC** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28632)
- **Authors (8, all NVIDIA Research, no other institutions listed):** Noam Rotstein, Yoad Tewel, Chen Tessler, Haggai Maron, Gal Chechik, Or Litany, Yoni Kasten, Yuval Atzmon — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)
- **Project page:** `research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/` — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)
- **Code/weights: NONE.** The project page carries **no GitHub link, no Hugging Face link, no "code coming soon" notice and no license** — only the paper, videos and BibTeX. The arXiv page likewise states nothing about release; its only outbound link is the project page — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/); [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28632)
- **What it does:** co-generates video and 3D motion for any supplied rig, plus a camera that registers the motion to the scene. Three usable directions: **Video-to-Motion** (recovers a subject's 3D motion from video **given the subject's rig in its rest pose**), **Motion-to-Video** (from rig + 3D motion + text prompt, with or without a reference first frame), and joint text→(video+motion). Also scene-aware animation from an input image and keyframe-controlled motion trajectories — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)
- **Architecture:** 3D motion stays in a **separate model supporting arbitrary skeletons**, linked to a **pretrained video model (not named)** by a small number of **cross-attention layers**; the motion branch can train on motion-only data; a **joint-localization head** predicts where joints appear in generated frames, and that is the mechanism for camera recovery — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/); [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28632)
- **Training data:** "Together with human and robot video, this yields nearly **200 K training pairs**"; a generative video editor placed animated 3D assets into scenes to cover many skeletons — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)
- **Evaluation:** the abstract reports only that the model "animates diverse rigs from text more faithfully than video-first pipelines." **No named metrics, no benchmark numbers, and no mention of GVHMR, TRAM or WHAM** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28632)
- **Not stated anywhere:** base video model name, output resolution, frame count, VRAM, runtime — [Source](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/)
- Independent coverage exists but adds no benchmarks: an AI-newsletter item and a blog post, both summarising the project page — [Source](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nvidia-jca-co-generates-3d-motion-and-video-for-any-rig); [Source](https://kenashe.ai/blog/2026-10-21-nvidias-jca-turns-generated-video-into-rig-motion)

**(b) ActionMesh — CONFIRMED, code released, but NOT a rigged skeleton**
- **Title:** "ActionMesh: Animated 3D Mesh Generation with Temporal 3D Diffusion"; **arXiv 2601.16148**, first submitted **January 2026**, **accepted to CVPR 2026** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16148); [CVPR PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Sabathier_ActionMesh_Animated_3D_Mesh_Generation_with_Temporal_3D_Diffusion_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- **Authors and affiliations — note this is NOT Meta alone:** Remy Sabathier, David Novotny, Niloy J. Mitra, Tom Monnier; **Meta Reality Labs + SpAItial + University College London** — [Source](https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.16148)
- **Code: YES**, `github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh`; project page `remysabathier.github.io/actionmesh/`; HF Space `huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/ActionMesh` — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)
- **Method:** extends 3D diffusion with a time dimension ("temporal 3D diffusion"). Stage 1 generates synchronised latents for a sequence of 3D shapes; stage 2 uses a **temporal autoencoder** to convert those shapes into **deformations of a reference mesh**. The arXiv v2 added more baselines and a larger evaluation on the authors' new **ActionBench** benchmark — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16148)
- **Output — this is the key finding:** by default **one `.glb` per frame** (`mesh_000.glb`, `mesh_001.glb`, …). With **Blender 3.5.1** installed it exports a single `animated_mesh.glb` with embedded animation, importable in Blender. With PyTorch3D it renders an `.mp4`. **The repo page does not say whether the animation is per-vertex or skeletal, and mentions no rig or skeleton output at all** — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)
- **Inputs:** `.mp4` or a folder of PNGs; **16 to 31 frames (default 16), extra frames ignored**; optional alpha masks (RMBG-1.4 removes background automatically otherwise); **SAM2 recommended** for isolating the subject on custom video. Mode 2 ({Video + 3D}→4D) takes an existing `.glb` and **preserves its topology and texture** — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)
- **Install:** `git clone`, `git submodule update --init --recursive`, `pip install -r requirements.txt`, `pip install -e .`. Optional PyTorch3D (rendered video and {video+3D}→4D) and **Blender 3.5.1** (single-GLB export). **Developed with torch 2.4.0, CUDA 12.1, torchvision 0.19.0.** Python version not stated — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)
- **VRAM and runtime (the only hard figures in this whole report):** "NVIDIA GPU with **32GB VRAM, or 12GB with `--low_ram` mode**" (e.g. Colab T4). On an **H100**: default ~**75 s** (higher quality), `--fast` ~**45 s** (slightly reduced quality). The "less than a minute" tagline matches only the fast mode; a blog's "45 seconds" figure is a third-party restatement of the `--fast` number — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh); [third-party](https://prompts.brightcoding.dev/blog/actionmesh-turn-any-video-into-animated-3d-in-45-seconds)
- **Weights on Hugging Face:** `facebook/ActionMesh`, plus dependencies `VAST-AI/TripoSG` (image-to-3D), `facebook/dinov2-large`, `briaai/RMBG-1.4` (background removal). `facebook/actionbench` is the evaluation **dataset**, not a model — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)
- **Humans:** the repo "does not say whether humans are supported or specially handled"; its examples are **a camel, a kangaroo, a panda and a spring**, and it refers generically to an "animated subject" — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)
- License: the repo points to a LICENSE.txt but the landing page does not name the license type — [Source](https://github.com/facebookresearch/actionmesh)

### Inferences
- **JCA is not usable in October 2026.** A week-old arXiv paper from a corporate lab with no code link, no license and no release notice is not an operator's tool. Its value right now is as the best available statement of where the field is going, and as validation that the "keep motion in motion space, use the video model as a renderer and supervisor" architecture is the one to bet on. Nothing in the briefing's description of JCA was wrong — it is simply unavailable.
- **ActionMesh does not solve the user's problem, and the misattribution matters.** It outputs a **vertex-animated mesh with no rig**. For retargeting to a character you need joint rotations; a per-frame vertex soup (or a single GLB with baked vertex animation) cannot be retargeted without first solving a rigging/skinning problem that ActionMesh explicitly sidesteps — the paper's selling point is "without rigging." Combined with the 16–31 frame cap (≈0.7–1.3 s at 24 fps) and animal/object examples, it is the wrong tool for human skeletal motion. It **is** a strong tool for a different job: turning a short clip of a non-humanoid subject into a deformable mesh, and its mode 2 preserving an input mesh's topology and texture is genuinely useful if you already have the asset.
- ActionMesh's `--low_ram` 12 GB mode and 45–75 s H100 runtimes are the only properly published resource figures in this entire research area, which is itself a comment on the state of documentation for the HMR repos.
- ActionMesh's hard dependency on **Blender 3.5.1** for the single-GLB export is a version pin worth noting — that is an old Blender, and the ComfyUI node packs in Q8 want Blender 4.0+. If both are on one machine, expect to keep two Blender installs.

### Gaps
- **JCA: no code, no weights, no license, no release timeline, no VRAM, no runtime, no benchmark numbers, base video model unnamed.** Every practical question about it is unanswerable today.
- **JCA reports no quantitative video→motion comparison against GVHMR/TRAM/WHAM**, so there is no way to know whether its video→motion direction is competitive with the models you can actually run.
- **ActionMesh: whether the animation is per-vertex or skeletal is not stated on the repo landing page**, and no rig output is mentioned. I am inferring "per-vertex / no rig" from the stage-2 description ("deformations of a reference mesh") and from the absence of any rig mention — this inference is strong but is not an explicit quote.
- ActionMesh's license type and Python version are unstated; whether humans work at all is unstated.

## Q8. ComfyUI integration: nodes for video→motion and motion→FBX

### Takeaway
There are **three real GVHMR-based node packs** plus **SAM 3D Body now in ComfyUI core**, and at least two of them genuinely export rigged animation — `ComfyUI-Video2MotionCapture` has an explicit `SaveSMPLAsRiggedFBX` node plus Mixamo and UE-mannequin retargeting, and core SAM 3D Body exports **BVH tested with Blender import**. The custom packs are all WIP-grade with license inconsistencies and a hard dependency on an embedded Blender/`bpy`.

### Cited Findings

**ComfyUI-Video2MotionCapture (AKASubaz)** — [Source](https://github.com/AKASubaz/ComfyUI-Video2MotionCapture)
- **7 nodes:** `LoadGVHMRModels`, `LoadMotionData`, `GVHMRInference`, **`SaveSMPLAsRiggedFBX`**, `RetargetSMPLToMixamo`, `RetargetsMixamoToUEMannequin`, `SaveSMPLAsMesh`
- **Install:** clone into `ComfyUI/custom_nodes`, `pip install -r requirements.txt` (for Portable, use the embedded Python or ComfyUI Manager), then `python install.py` to download models. **The README's `cd` path uses a different folder name than the repo** — a real install trap.
- **Models:** `install.py` fetches GVHMR + ViTPose + SMPL, **~3.5 GB total**, into `ComfyUI/models/motion_capture`
- **Rigged FBX: yes** — `SaveSMPLAsRiggedFBX` "creates a rigged SMPL mesh from params and saves as FBX", and the README says it **fixes axis orientation automatically**
- **Blender:** uses an **embedded Blender worker** for skinning, exports and retargeting; nodes try to use `bpy` via pip (usually automatic on Windows; **on Linux/Mac you may need a system Blender install or a `bpy` build**); **Blender 4.0+ recommended** for retargeting; retargeting logic references **Rokoko Studio**
- **Requirements:** ComfyUI (Standard or Portable), **Python 3.10+**, Blender 4.0+. VRAM and torch version **not stated**
- **Known issues:** title labels it **"WIP"**; duplicate bone names (e.g. Wrist and Hand both mapping to Mixamo Hand) handled automatically; a troubleshooting entry covers `bpy.ops` RuntimeErrors which **may require a valid display** or a correct `bpy` install; **README says MIT but the sidebar shows GPL-3.0**; 6 open issues; 17 commits; no last-commit date shown

**ComfyUI-MotionCapture (PozzettiAndrea)** — [Source](https://github.com/PozzettiAndrea/ComfyUI-MotionCapture)
- ComfyUI wrapper for **GVHMR-based** 3D human motion capture from video; extracts SMPL parameters and 3D skeletal motion
- **Install, three routes:** (1) ComfyUI Manager, search "MotionCapture", install the highest version, try nightly if that fails; (2) Manager → Install via Git URL; (3) manual: clone into `ComfyUI/custom_nodes`, `pip install -r requirements.txt --upgrade`, `python install.py`
- **Carries an explicit warning** that it uses the experimental `comfy-env` package "to attempt a one click isolated install, which will download and use **pixi** package manager"; config files `comfy-env-root.toml`, `comfy-test.toml`
- **Repo license GPL-3.0**; vendors GVHMR, ViTPose, HMR2, SMPL, SMPL-X; SMPL/SMPL-X **require separate licenses from their providers**
- **244 commits** — by far the most active of the packs — 16 open issues; **no node names, no VRAM, no export format documented on the landing page**; a demo filename references **"smpl_to_bvh"**, which implies BVH export but the page does not describe it
- A third-party node index describes its workflow as **video → SAM3 → GVHMR → SMPL params**, with `SMPLToFBX` and `SMPLToGLB` in its node list, and lists an `SMPLRetargetToSMPL` node — **but neither the repo page nor the SAM3 repo page confirms that SAM3 is wired in**, so treat the SAM3 step and the node names as unconfirmed — [Source](https://comfyai.run/custom_node/ComfyUI-MotionCapture); [Source](https://comfyai.run/documentation/SMPLRetargetToSMPL)

**ComfyUI core: SAM 3D Body** — [Source](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-23-sam-3d-body-native-comfyui)
- Merged into **core** via Comfy-Org/ComfyUI **PR #14370** on **2026-08-23** (Kijai's integration), **no custom node pack required**
- Nodes: `SAM3DBody_Loader`, `SAM3DBody_Predict`, `SAM3DBody_Render`, **`SAM3DBody_Smooth`** (temporal smoothing for stable pose across video frames), `SAM3DBody_FaceExpression`; plus `LoadMoGeModel` + `MoGeGeometryToFOV` for camera FoV, and `SAM3_VideoTrack` for multi-person via SAM 3.1 tracks
- Weights into **`ComfyUI/models/detection/`**: `sam_3d_body_dinov3_bf16.safetensors` or `sam_3d_body_dinov3_int8_convrot.safetensors` (Comfy-Org repackaged on HF; the article also links Meta's original checkpoint)
- **Exports: GLB** (body mesh, or OpenPose/SCAIL-pose custom meshes, as 3D animation) and **BVH** (animation, **"tested with Blender import"**)
- A separate `ComfyUI-SAM3DBody` pack also exposes a **"SAM 3D Body: Save Skeleton"** node — [Source](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI-SAM3DBody/sam3d-body-save-skeleton)
- **VRAM not stated; required ComfyUI version not stated** (you need a build at or after the 2026-08-23 merge); **no limitations listed**

**Other relevant packs**
- **`comfyui_controlnet_aux`** supplies the **DWPose** estimator, used by the official Wan 2.2 Animate workflow to convert input video into pose and facial control sequences — [Source](https://comfyui-wiki.com//en/tutorial/advanced/video/wan2.2/wan2-2-animate)
- **ComfyUI-HyMotion (Aero-Ex)** — **text**-to-3D-human-motion based on HY-Motion 1.0 (not video-based). Exports **FBX with skeleton and textures**, or NPZ. Requires the **`fbxsdkpy` package from a custom index**. To consume GVHMR output it needs an **SMPL-H → HY-Motion conversion step** — [Source](https://github.com/Aero-Ex/ComfyUI-HyMotion)
- **ComfyUI-MotionDiff (Fannovel16)** — implementations of **4DHuman, MotionGPT, MDM, MotionDiffuse and ReMoDiffuse** in ComfyUI — [Source](https://github.com/Fannovel16/ComfyUI-MotionDiff)
- A third-party ComfyUI node index lists `ComfyUI-HyMotion`'s `hy-motion-smpl-to-data` node, confirming an SMPL ingest path exists in that pack — [Source](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI-HyMotion/hy-motion-smpl-to-data)

### Inferences
- **The answer to "do they actually export rigged animation" is yes, with caveats.** `SaveSMPLAsRiggedFBX` plus the two retarget nodes is a complete video→FBX→Mixamo/UE path on paper, and it is the only pack that documents retargeting. But it does its skinning and export by driving an embedded Blender through `bpy`, which means the pack's reliability is really Blender-headless reliability — hence the documented `bpy.ops` RuntimeError that "may require a valid display". On a headless Linux box expect to need Xvfb or a proper `bpy` wheel.
- **For a first install, core SAM 3D Body is the right starting point** (no custom nodes, no GPL/MIT ambiguity, no pixi, BVH tested in Blender), accepting that it is a per-frame model with smoothing rather than a world-frame video model. Use `ComfyUI-MotionCapture` (244 commits, most active) or `ComfyUI-Video2MotionCapture` (documented rigged-FBX + retarget nodes) when you need GVHMR's world-frame output.
- **BVH is the better interchange target than FBX here.** BVH is joint rotations only — exactly what a retarget needs — and both core SAM 3D Body and (apparently) `ComfyUI-MotionCapture`'s `smpl_to_bvh` produce it, whereas FBX from these packs carries an SMPL mesh and skin weights you will immediately discard.
- The `ComfyUI-Video2MotionCapture` README/sidebar license contradiction (MIT vs GPL-3.0) is explained by it vendoring GVHMR, which `ComfyUI-MotionCapture` states is **GPL-3.0**. Irrelevant for personal use, but it tells you both packs carry GVHMR source inline rather than installing it, so expect to debug GVHMR itself through a node wrapper.
- `ComfyUI-HyMotion` + `ComfyUI-MotionDiff` together mean the motion-*generation* side is also in ComfyUI, so a text→motion→(retarget) path exists entirely in-graph without any video at all. For many actions that is a faster route than generating video and extracting from it, and it skips every problem in Q2.

### Gaps
- **`ComfyUI-MotionCapture`'s node names, export formats, VRAM and SAM3 dependency are not documented on its repo landing page.** The node names (`SMPLToFBX`, `SMPLToGLB`, `SMPLRetargetToSMPL`) and the SAM3 step come only from a third-party node index and are unconfirmed against the repo.
- **No last-commit dates** could be read for either custom pack (both show only a "© 2026" footer), so I cannot say whether either is currently maintained.
- **No VRAM figure for any ComfyUI motion node.**
- **No independent report on output quality** — no r/StableDiffusion, r/comfyui or ComfyUI Discord writeup, and no YouTube walkthrough, surfaced in any of my searches for any of these packs. I have **no evidence from a user** that the FBX export produces animation that is actually usable in Blender/Unreal, only the READMEs' claims. The one social-media item found was a Threads post reproducing a pack's own feature list — [Source](https://www.threads.com/@4kmal4lif/post/DU-hR_LgUD5/video-comfy-ui-custom-node-package-built-around-gvhmr-based-d-human-motion-capture)
- **Dedicated SMPL/SMPL-X viewer or editor nodes** (beyond export) were not found.
- The required minimum ComfyUI version for core SAM 3D Body is unstated.

## Cross-cutting notes for the report writer

- **The single most useful artefact in this research is the OnlineHMR comparison table** (Q1). It is the only place I found where WHAM, TRAM, GVHMR, PromptHMR and Human3R are measured by one group, on one protocol, for both camera-frame and world-frame metrics *and* speed on a named GPU. Everything else in the accuracy literature is authors comparing against their own re-runs.
- **Documentation quality is the binding constraint in this field, not model quality.** Of ~12 HMR models surveyed, exactly two publish a VRAM figure (Human3R 8 GB self-reported, ActionMesh 32 GB / 12 GB low-ram). None of GVHMR, TRAM, WHAM, PromptHMR, CameraHMR or SMPLer-X does. An operator's manual has to tell the reader to measure, because the numbers do not exist.
- **Two of the user's briefing items were directionally right but materially wrong in detail:** JCA is real but unavailable (no code/weights/license as of 2026-10-27, one week after submission); ActionMesh is real and released but is Meta+SpAItial+UCL rather than Meta alone, outputs an unrigged animated mesh rather than a skeleton, caps at 16–31 frames, and demonstrates on animals and objects rather than humans.
- **The three claims in the assignment I could not substantiate at all,** and which should be presented as open questions rather than quietly filled in: (1) that frame interpolation or temporal super-resolution helps 3D pose on fast motion; (2) that there is a documented keypoint-refitting recipe with concrete settings for fast-limb under-shoot; (3) that pose-controlled video models can act as a motion cleaner or amplifier. For (1) and (3) the literature is silent; for (2) the literature actively warns that naive 2D-reprojection refitting increases depth error (KITRO) and destabilises at limb terminations (ResiHMR).
