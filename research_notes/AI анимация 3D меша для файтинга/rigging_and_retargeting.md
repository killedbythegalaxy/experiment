# Auto-Rigging and Motion Retargeting for 3D Character Meshes — State of the Toolchain, October 2026

> Scope note: all pricing and product-status claims below are date-stamped to the source that carried them. Where only vendor marketing was available, it is labelled as such. Several sub-questions could not be answered from primary sources and are listed in the Gaps sections — those are real holes, not omissions.

---

## Q1. Auto-rigging / auto-skinning tools in 2026: what exists, what it costs, what quality it actually produces

### Takeaway
Mixamo is still online and free but functionally frozen and officially bipedal-humanoid-only; the practical 2026 quality leaders for an arbitrary humanoid mesh are Tripo's auto-rig (best UE5 retarget compatibility and the only one that handled a non-humanoid in an independent test), Reallusion AccuRig (best skin weights, free, A-pose-only, humanoid-only), and the open-source UniRig (SIGGRAPH 2025, template-free, handles non-humanoids, but a local GPU pipeline rather than a product). Every one of them omits facial joints and IK pre-setup, and none of them produce capes, weapons or hitbox sockets.

### Cited Findings

**Independent comparative test (the single most useful non-vendor source found)**
- A head-to-head test dated **May 14, 2026** rigged a ~30K-tri single-material humanoid mesh with Tripo, Meshy, Cascadeur, AccuRig and Mixamo, then imported each into **Blender 5.1** and **Unreal Engine 5.7** and retargeted to Manny/Quinn, running walk/run/upper-body-wave for 30 s on a moving platform under the **Mover 2.0** third-person template — [StraySpark, AI Auto-Rigging Showdown 2026](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)
- **Tripo (Tripo P1.0)**: standard biped skeleton, bone names "match Manny/Quinn closely"; cleanest UE 5.7 retarget, needing only a foot-bone offset tweak; no visible jitter under Mover 2.0; ~3 minutes end-to-end. Failure modes: shoulder and chest weights **over-smoothed**, causing mesh slumping during arm raises on broad shoulder pads; **jaw weights noisy**; **no facial joints and no IK chain pre-setup**. Rigged a stylized robot with sensible bone placement and no manual fixes — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)
- **Meshy**: non-standard spine and clavicle bone names, so the UE retargeter does **not** auto-detect those chains and manual re-mapping is required; hip/pelvis weighting inconsistent with **some hip pivots sitting 5–15 cm from where a hand-rigger would place them**, producing foot-IK mismatch and feet sliding under Mover 2.0; ~8 minutes — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)
- **Reallusion AccuRig**: expects **A-pose** meshes facing a specific direction; **non-humanoid characters not supported**; weight painting described as "tight" with bone placement matching a skilled rigger; naming closer to Manny than Meshy's but not as close as Tripo's; clean in UE 5.7 once scale is set, though one character imported at **0.01× scale** and needed a fix; **free with a Reallusion account**; ~10 minutes including the scale fix — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)
- **Mixamo**: humanoid mesh under a "fairly tight set of mesh conventions"; Mixamo-specific bone names; **best-documented retarget path into UE, with built-in Mixamo→Manny support**; stock animations "subtly dated"; free; ~12 minutes — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)
- **Cascadeur**: not an auto-rigger in the relevant sense — it "expects an existing rig rather than generating one from a mesh"; its auto-rig is "OK but not great"; it produced the **highest animation export quality** in the test with retarget-ready bone names; ~30 minutes per useful 2-second pose-to-pose animation — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)

**UniRig (open-source, SIGGRAPH 2025)**
- Official implementation of "One Model to Rig Them All: Diverse Skeleton Rigging with UniRig", SIGGRAPH 2025 / ACM TOG, developed by **Tsinghua University and Tripo (VAST)**, released April 2025 — [GitHub: VAST-AI-Research/UniRig](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig); paper [arXiv:2504.12451](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12451v1)
- Two-stage method: a GPT-like transformer autoregressively predicts a **topologically valid skeleton hierarchy** via a **Skeleton Tree Tokenization** scheme; a second stage uses **Bone-Point Cross Attention** to predict per-vertex skinning weights and bone attributes (including attributes used for physics simulation) — [arXiv:2504.12451](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12451v1)
- The paper's comparison table lists UniRig as **template-free and multi-category**, at **1–5 s** inference cost versus about **2 minutes** for Tripo's own method — [arXiv:2504.12451](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12451v1)
- Model card states it targets humans, animals, fictional characters, and inorganic structures — [HuggingFace VAST-AI/UniRig](https://huggingface.co/VAST-AI/UniRig)
- Follow-up work **SkinTokens** is announced in the README: it unifies skeleton and skinning into a single autoregressive sequence via learned discrete skin tokens, adding reinforcement learning and a compression module — [GitHub README](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig)
- **Release caveat:** an early HuggingFace README stated the skinning-weight prediction model and the models trained on the Rig-XL/VRoid datasets described in the paper "will be released separately at a later date" — [HuggingFace README revision](https://huggingface.co/VAST-AI/UniRig/blame/9efc1537465a93cee7d244bb078cab84bf762d7b/README.md). I could not confirm from the sources retrieved whether those weights are now public as of Oct 2026 — see Gaps.
- Related 2025 research on the same problem: **Auto-Connect: Connectivity-Preserving RigFormer with Direct Preference Optimization** — [arXiv:2506.11430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11430)

### Inferences
- The field has bifurcated: **bone-naming convention compatibility now matters more than skinning quality** for engine work. Tripo won the independent test mainly because its bone names fuzzy-match Manny/Quinn, letting UE's auto chain detection work; Meshy lost mostly on naming, not on weights. For a UE5 target, naming convention is the first thing to check on any candidate tool.
- Every tool in the independent test produced **no facial joints**. For a fighting game that needs facial expression (hit reactions, taunts), auto-rig output is a starting skeleton only; face rigging remains a manual or separate-tool step.
- The 5–15 cm hip-pivot error observed for Meshy is a direct cause of foot-IK failure. Hip/pelvis pivot placement is the highest-leverage thing to verify and hand-fix after any auto-rig, because the pelvis is also the retarget root in both UE and Unity.
- AccuRig's A-pose requirement versus Mixamo's "default/neutral pose" requirement means the authoring decision (which rest pose to generate the mesh in) constrains which auto-riggers you can use later. Generating in A-pose keeps both AccuRig and Mixamo available; T-pose-only meshes lose AccuRig.
- UniRig is the only credible route for stylized/non-humanoid proportions **without** a template skeleton, but it is a research codebase with an unverified weight-release status, so it carries integration risk a studio must budget for.

## Q2. Retargeting: the engine workflows, the research frontier, and exactly what breaks

### Takeaway
UE5's IK Rig + IK Retargeter is the most capable and best-documented path, but since UE 5.6 it requires an **Op Stack** — without one the target simply does not animate — and root motion will not transfer unless a root chain is defined in **both** IK Rigs and mapped. Unity's Humanoid avatar retargeting is far more automatic but silently discards the root bone in favour of the hips, which is the root cause of most Unity root-motion complaints. The 2026 research frontier has moved from skeleton-specific to topology-agnostic retargeting, with one paper arguing the whole cross-skeleton objective is formally non-identifiable.

### Cited Findings

**Unreal Engine 5 — official documentation (page header reads UE 5.8)**
- Prerequisites: two skeletal meshes, and **IK Rig assets for both with retarget chains defined in each** — [Epic: IK Rig Animation Retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- Workflow: Content Browser **Add (+) > Animation > IK Rig > IK Retargeter** → set source/target IK Rigs and preview meshes in Asset Settings → **Run Retarget** → preview in the Asset Browser → export selected animations to the target skeleton — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Chains, not bones:** chains are defined by start and end bones, so source and target may have different joint counts within a chain. Each chain must exist on both rigs. Chains can be created with **Auto Create Retarget Chains** or via **New Retarget Chain…** in the Hierarchy panel — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Chain names are matched by fuzzy string comparison** (`ArmLeft` can match `left_arm`); names are auto-assigned from common bone keywords (arm, leg, spine, head…); **Left/Right prefixes are derived from the bones' average X position**; duplicates get numeric suffixes — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Retarget root / pelvis:** set by right-clicking the bone in the IK Rig Editor Hierarchy and choosing **Set Pelvis**, on both rigs. "The pelvis is used so root motion transfers proportionally between characters." — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Root motion:** runtime retargeting copies root motion from the source. "Ignore Root Lock in Preview" affects **only** the editor preview; export behaviour is controlled by **ExportRootLockMode** — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Op Stack:** a list of modular operations evaluated **top-down** on a selected chain; ops can be reordered, some contain sub-stacks; each op supports custom LOD thresholds to skip costly steps at higher LODs; **custom ops can be written in C++** — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Pose matching:** mismatched reference poses (A-pose source vs T-pose target) reduce accuracy; fix by editing a Retarget Pose in Edit Mode or entering exact rotations in Details. Poses can be imported from Animation Sequences or Pose Assets and exported as Pose Assets. **Auto Align** matches bones by direction, local or global axes, or mesh principal axis; **Snap Character to Ground** adjusts height — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Override Sets and Variables:** override sets vary op properties and enable/disable ops without changing the base asset, assigned at runtime on the **Retarget Pose From Mesh** node; variables are exposed as input pins and require an active override set to take effect; anim curves can drive override properties. **Variables are single-value only — "You cannot use arrays, maps, or sets."** — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- **Stated limitations:** chains must be defined on both rigs; name-similarity matching may need manual adjustment, with incompatibilities reported in the log as warnings or errors; local and global axis alignment "may produce nonsensical results on skeletons with different rotation directions"; ops are evaluated only on the chains they're assigned to — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)

**UE 5.6-specific practitioner findings (community, secondhand — flagged as such)**
- In the 5.6 retargeter, **"the target won't animate without an op stack."** The reported setup is to press **Add Default Ops**, then modify, then add a **Root Motion op** — [spaceplace.gibsonmartelli.com, Jun 9 2025](https://spaceplace.gibsonmartelli.com/2025/06/09/retarget-rootmotion-anims-from-older-skeleton-to-default-quinn-skeleton-with-rootmotion/)
- **Op order matters: the Root Motion op must sit lower in the list than the Pelvis op** — "probably the execution order is top to bottom" — [same source](https://spaceplace.gibsonmartelli.com/2025/06/09/retarget-rootmotion-anims-from-older-skeleton-to-default-quinn-skeleton-with-rootmotion/)
- A **Z-drift bug in long clips** (pelvis slowly rising in Z over time) was fixed by removing the Pelvis bone from the spine chain, making a **new chain containing only the Pelvis**, then setting that chain's **FK Rotation Mode to one-to-one and Translation Mode to Absolute** — [same source](https://spaceplace.gibsonmartelli.com/2025/06/09/retarget-rootmotion-anims-from-older-skeleton-to-default-quinn-skeleton-with-rootmotion/)
- **"If the root chain is not defined in both IK Rigs and mapped in the retargeter, root motion displacement will not transfer — the retargeted animation will play in place even when the source clip has root displacement."** — [StraySpark, UE retargeting guide](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/unreal-engine-animation-retargeting-ik-rig-complete-guide)
- Guides **disagree on the retarget root**: one says the hips/pelvis are "almost always correct" because that bone carries overall world translation; a 5.5-era tutorial instead uses the skeleton root — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/unreal-engine-animation-retargeting-ik-rig-complete-guide) vs [QuodSoler](https://www.quodsoler.com/blog/mastering-ik-retargeting-how-to-efficiently-share-animations-in-unreal-engine)
- For Mixamo→MetaHuman, enable **Enable Root Motion** and **Root Motion from Everything** on the retargeted animation asset, then on the AnimBlueprint; the same guide notes that using the pelvis as retarget root to *simulate* root motion is less reliable — [yelzkizi](https://yelzkizi.org/retarget-mixamo-animations-to-metahuman/)
- **Height scaling:** root translation may need scaling by the height ratio, with movement-component speed matched; "root motion animations need the former; in-place animations driven by a movement component need the latter" — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/unreal-engine-animation-retargeting-ik-rig-complete-guide)
- An older UE5 forum thread confirms **Absolute translation mode keeps the character in place** — [Epic Developer Community Forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/retargeting-root-bone-ue5/506512)

**Unity Humanoid — what breaks (all community sources; no official Unity doc found)**
- **Root bone is dropped and hips become root.** Humanoid rigging can discard a parent root bone and use the hips instead, subtly changing position and rotation; a documented workaround is to export the rig as a Generic FBX and reimport as Humanoid — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/root-motion-doesnt-follow-properly-the-animation/818669)
- **Root orientation drift:** characters turn or veer during run cycles when the animation's root isn't facing forward on its starting frame; fixes are toggling the clip's root-orientation setting between "Original"/"Body Orientation" or **baking rotation into pose** — [Unity Forum](https://forum.unity.com/threads/how-to-make-a-correct-humanoid-root-motion.451281/)
- **Accumulated yaw bias:** one reported case had run-cycle root rotation shifting direction every loop; offsetting the root transform rotation fixed straight running but not hard turns, and baking into pose was unacceptable because gameplay scripts still needed root rotation — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/strange-rotation-when-using-humanoid-avatar/703721)
- **Foot drift from retargeting:** foot-pinning edits that look stable in the authoring tool shift after export; attributed to Humanoid retargeting, with **Mecanim IK** recommended as the fix — [Soxware support](https://support.soxware.com/communities/1/topics/316-questions-regarding-humanoid-root-motion)
- **Most recent datapoint (October 2026):** a UnityGLTF issue reports root motion working for non-humanoid models but **not** for humanoid ones, the poster attributing it to humanoid clips being imported as generic clips with **no motion curves**, and noting the fix may be blocked because Unity's clip-generation code is hidden — [UnityGLTF issue #916](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF/issues/916)
- A regression report says root motion broke in **Unity 2022.3.16f1** but not 2021, with the reporter's workaround being to disable **Apply Root Motion** on the Animator and a fix stated to have landed in a dev branch — [Oculus Unity-Movement issue #51](https://github.com/oculus-samples/Unity-Movement/issues/51)

**AI/learning-based retargeting research, 2026**
- **Retargeting Motions to Diverse Skeletons via Learnable Flattening** (Yang, Sinz, Pierzchlewicz; submitted 29 Sep 2026): a Transformer autoencoder with a **skeleton-topology-agnostic latent space**; reports a **43–47% reduction in zero-shot global joint position error** and a user study with 37 participants — [arXiv:2609.38578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38578)
- **Skinned Motion Retargeting via Artifact-driven Kinematic Prior Refinement** (Hong, Kim, Jang, Choi, Noh; submitted 6 Sep 2026): targets **self-penetration and other geometric artifacts on the target mesh**; listed as accepted at **SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 (Journal Track)** — [arXiv:2609.06517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06517)
- **Why Cross-Skeleton Retargeting Is Non-Identifiable: Structural Limits of Generative Motion Models** (Li, Yang, Marttinen, Pajarinen; submitted 29 Sep 2026): a theoretical/diagnostic paper arguing current cross-skeleton retargeting objectives **can fail to learn the source-conditioned mapping they claim** — [arXiv:2609.37297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37297v1)
- **ReActor: Reinforcement Learning for Physics-Aware Motion Retargeting** — [arXiv:2605.06593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06593)
- **Retargeting Matters: General Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Motion Tracking** — [arXiv:2510.02252](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02252)
- Lineage: **SAME** (Lee et al., 2023) is cited by the 2026 Hong et al. paper as an early skeleton-agnostic method; **HuMoT** (topology-agnostic transformers for retargeting) is [arXiv:2305.18897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18897); **Skeleton-free Pose Transfer for Stylized 3D Characters** is from 2022, [arXiv:2208.00790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.00790)

- **MotionBuilder remains the common industry tool for mocap cleanup and retargeting**, and "most studios mix mocap with keyframe work"; mocap and keyframe animation have "subtly different energy levels," so manual timing adjustment is needed to blend them — [MocapOnline, MoCap vs Keyframe](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/game-character-animation-guide)

### Inferences
- The UE5 pipeline's **two independent root-motion gates** are the main trap: (1) a root chain defined and mapped in both IK Rigs, and (2) a Root Motion op present in the Op Stack and ordered below the Pelvis op. Missing either produces the same visible symptom — animation plays in place — so diagnosis is ambiguous without checking both.
- Epic's **Left/Right chain prefixes are inferred from average X position**, which means a mesh authored facing −X, or a non-humanoid with asymmetric limb layout, will get silently mirrored chain assignments. This is a concrete, under-documented mirroring hazard for stylized characters.
- Unity's hips-as-root substitution is structurally incompatible with frame-exact root motion for a fighting game. The practical consequence is that a Unity fighting game should use **Generic** rigs with an explicit root bone and drive movement from gameplay code, not Humanoid + Apply Root Motion. The Oct 2026 UnityGLTF issue suggests this has not been fixed.
- The non-identifiability paper is important context for anyone considering an AI retargeter in a shipping pipeline: it argues the problem as currently posed is formally underdetermined, which is consistent with learned retargeters producing plausible-but-not-faithful motion — exactly the failure mode a fighting game cannot tolerate.

- **No official Unity documentation** on Humanoid root-motion behaviour or 2026 release notes was retrieved; every Unity finding above is a community report, several from older threads.

- **Reallusion iClone** retargeting and **Rokoko Blender plugin** retargeting workflows were not covered by any retrieved source. The Rokoko plugin in particular was not found at all.
- **Unity Animation Rigging package** specifics were not retrieved.
- Additive layers and mirroring failure modes were not addressed by any source found; only the X-position-based chain-prefix inference above touches mirroring.
- No source quantified how well the 2026 learned retargeters work on **stylized/non-human proportions** at production quality.

## Q3. The target chain: fighter mesh → rig → AI motion → FBX → UE5/Unity, and the SMPL licensing question

### Takeaway
The only publicly documented, production-grade SMPL-X → game-engine retargeting pipeline is Max Planck's own **BEDLAM 2.0 retargeting tool**, which runs SMPL-X `.npz` → FBX → Unreal's IK Retargeter with **IK explicitly disabled** and a separate floor-adjustment pass — and that design tells you where the pain is. On licensing, the situation changed materially in 2026: **SMPL's commercial rights sit with Max-Planck-Gesellschaft, commercial licensing was routed through Meshcapade, and Meshcapade was acquired by Epic Games in February 2026 with its online platforms shut down as of April 18**. AMASS — the dataset nearly every AI motion model trains on — is **non-commercial only and non-redistributable**.

### Cited Findings

**BEDLAM 2.0: the reference SMPL-X → UE pipeline (primary source)**
- Built on Epic's IK Retargeter; requires **Unreal Engine 5.3 or 5.4** (use the matching repo branch); UE plugins needed: **Python, Python Foundation Packages (numpy, for `.npz`), Python Remote Execution**; plus the **SMPL-X Blender add-on version 20241129 or later** for FBX export, and a Python env from `requirements.txt` — [GitHub: PerceivingSystems/bedlam2_retargeting](https://github.com/PerceivingSystems/bedlam2_retargeting)
- Assets: `Widgets/HumanEngineWidget` (editor utility widget GUI), `paths.json`, an **example SMPL-X IK Rig** (credited to Joachim Tesch), `sample.csv` source→target pairing file, and **`SMPLX_NEUTRAL.npz` in `body_models`** required for floor adjustment — using **the SMPL-X model with the head bun removed** — [BEDLAM 2.0 repo](https://github.com/PerceivingSystems/bedlam2_retargeting)
- Exported `.npz` fields: `gender`, `neutral`, `mocap_frame_rate` (**30.0**), `betas`, `poses` (**frames × 165**), `trans` (frames × 3). A 165-value pose array is consistent with **55 joints × 3 axis-angle values** (this arithmetic is inference, not stated on the page) — [BEDLAM 2.0 repo](https://github.com/PerceivingSystems/bedlam2_retargeting)
- Concrete steps: (1) export source animations and target bodies from `.npz` to `.fbx` with `make_fbx_files.py`, target bodies using `--tpose`; build the pairing CSV with `make_csv_file.py`. (2) Import FBX into UE with `import_batch.py` or the widget — **"Animations and skeletal meshes must sit at floor level"** and **"Keep IK disabled in the IK-Retargeter."** (3) Retarget with `retarget_batch.py` across batches/processes or via the widget; output to `retargeting/`. (4) Export retargeted sequences as `.fbx` (with or without mesh) and/or `.npz` (with or without betas) from the widget. (5) Run `adjust_floor_npz.py`, writing to `{input_dir}_floor_adjusted` — [BEDLAM 2.0 repo](https://github.com/PerceivingSystems/bedlam2_retargeting)
- Constraints stated or directly implied: **FK-only operation with IK disabled**; mismatched chain names require manual mapping; floor adjustment requires the specific head-bun-removed neutral model; setup is pinned to UE 5.3/5.4 and a specific add-on version; batch commands use Windows-style paths and the widget requires manual steps — [BEDLAM 2.0 repo](https://github.com/PerceivingSystems/bedlam2_retargeting)

### Inferences
- **The procedural chain, assembled from the sources above, for a fighter mesh → UE5:**
  1. Author/generate the mesh as a **single connected watertight mesh in A-pose** (A-pose keeps AccuRig and Mixamo both viable), transforms applied, **at the world origin**, no props/hair/cape/weapon geometry attached, nothing else in the file (Mixamo FAQ requirements; the Cinevva general guidance matches).
  2. Auto-rig. For a UE5 target, prefer a tool whose bone names fuzzy-match Manny/Quinn — Tripo did in the May 2026 test; AccuRig was next; Meshy needed manual chain re-mapping.
  3. **Hand-fix the pelvis/hip pivot first.** It is both the skinning problem (5–15 cm errors observed) and the retarget root in both engines.
  4. Build an **IK Rig** for the new skeleton: define retarget chains (Auto Create Retarget Chains, then verify Left/Right assignment, which Epic derives from average X position), and **Set Pelvis**.
  5. Build an **IK Retargeter**: source rig = the AI-motion skeleton (SMPL-X rig, Mixamo rig, or UE5 Manny), target = your rig. Fix the **Retarget Pose** if source and target rest poses differ (A vs T), using Auto Align and Snap Character to Ground.
  6. In UE 5.6+, **Add Default Ops** in the Op Stack — without an Op Stack nothing animates — then add a **Root Motion op below the Pelvis op**.
  7. For long clips, if the pelvis drifts in Z, split the pelvis into its own chain with **FK Rotation Mode = one-to-one, Translation Mode = Absolute**.
  8. Run Retarget, export to the target skeleton, then set **Enable Root Motion** + **Root Motion from Everything** on each animation asset and on the AnimBlueprint. Note **ExportRootLockMode** governs export, not the preview checkbox.
  9. If the source is SMPL-X specifically, follow BEDLAM 2.0's hard-won constraints: meshes and animations **at floor level**, **IK disabled in the retargeter** (FK-only), and a **separate floor-adjustment pass afterwards**.
- **Why BEDLAM disables IK** is the most informative detail in this whole area: Max Planck's own engineers, retargeting SMPL-X to varied body shapes in UE, chose FK-only plus a post-hoc floor correction rather than trusting the retargeter's IK. For a fighting game that needs exact foot planting, this implies foot contact should be solved as a **separate, explicit pass** (either post-process or in-engine Mecanim IK / UE Full Body IK), not as a retargeter setting.
- **The licensing conclusion for a commercial fighting game:** if you (a) ship no SMPL/SMPL-X model files, (b) use no AMASS-trained-on-AMASS weights where the licence reaches, and (c) your final deliverable is baked FBX animation on *your own* skeleton, you have removed SMPL from the product. But the MPG license's "may not be used to train methods or neural networks for commercial use" clause reaches further than most people assume, and it means **an AI motion generator that was trained on SMPL/AMASS is itself legally contaminated for commercial use** unless its authors hold a commercial license. This, not the FBX output, is the real exposure for anyone using off-the-shelf text-to-motion models.
- **The Epic acquisition makes the licensing path genuinely uncertain right now.** Commercial SMPL licensing was routed through Meshcapade; Meshcapade's platforms are shut down as of April 18, 2026; MPG still holds the rights and still lists smpl@max-planck-innovation.de. The defensible action is to write to Max Planck Innovation directly rather than rely on the Meshcapade route described in older documentation.

## Q4. Fighting-game-specific requirements and how fighting-game studios actually author animation

### Takeaway
The best-documented fighting-game animation philosophy — Arc System Works on Guilty Gear Xrd — is the direct opposite of what an AI mocap pipeline produces: the team **deliberately rejected full animation, turned off keyframe interpolation, and used held frames with stronger poses**, because smooth interpolated motion "made the visuals appear more 3D." Capcom does use mocap (publicly, for SF6's Jamie), and general industry practice mixes mocap with keyframe cleanup in MotionBuilder, with an estimated 150–300 unique clips per fighter. I found **no primary source at all** on how frame data (startup/active/recovery), hitboxes or cancel windows are authored at any studio — that is a hard information gap.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **The Xrd approach is actively hostile to AI motion pipelines.** AI motion generators and mocap both produce densely sampled, smoothly interpolated per-frame rotation data. Xrd's stated method requires the opposite: a sparse set of strong poses with interpolation off. A mocap or AI-generated clip would have to be *reduced* to stepped keys at authored intervals, which is a creative decision per move, not a conversion you can automate. For a stylized/anime fighting game, AI motion is at best a reference layer for a human animator, not a deliverable.
- **Mocap's role in fighting games is realistic-style only.** Capcom's public SF6 mocap and the industry pattern of mocap + MotionBuilder cleanup are consistent with a realistic art direction. Arc System Works' documented rejection of full animation is consistent with an anime art direction. The choice of whether AI motion is usable at all is therefore downstream of the art direction, not of the technology.
- The 150–300 clips/fighter estimate, if roughly right, explains why studios invest in mocap at all for realistic fighters, and simultaneously why frame-exactness is a tooling problem: a studio is managing thousands of clips whose frame counts are gameplay-balance data.
- **The absence of sourced information on frame-data authoring is itself a finding.** Across all searches, the only window into how a shipped fighting game stores move timing was *fan reverse-engineering* (SF6's MotionKey/MotionID/motlist structure; Strive's extractable animation files; TFH's in-game frame data display). No studio has publicly documented its move-authoring tooling in anything retrieved. A developer building a fighting game should expect to build this layer themselves, and the SF6 structure — a key that fires a named animation from a list at a specific frame — is the clearest available model of what that layer looks like.
- Nothing in the retrieved sources supports or contradicts the premise that auto-rigs fail on hitbox sockets, weapon sockets, or 60 fps determinism. Mechanically, however: auto-riggers in the May 2026 test produced **no IK chain pre-setup and no facial joints**, and none of the vendors claims socket/attachment-point generation, so every gameplay attachment point (hitbox volumes, weapon sockets, VFX spawn points) is necessarily manual work after auto-rigging.

### Gaps

## Q5. The fully offline open-source stack: what is actually possible in Blender + Python in 2026

### Takeaway
A complete offline chain exists — **UniRig (local GPU) or Rigify/Auto-Rig Pro for rigging, Auto-Rig Pro Remap or Blender constraints for retargeting, with Blender's FBX exporter and engine presets at the end** — and the paid piece is only $50. The quality ceiling is set by two things the offline tools do not solve: pelvis-pivot accuracy after auto-rigging, and foot-contact correction after retargeting.

### Cited Findings
- **UniRig runs locally** as an open-source implementation (GitHub + HuggingFace weights), is template-free and multi-category, and reports **1–5 s** per rig — [GitHub: VAST-AI-Research/UniRig](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig); [arXiv:2504.12451](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12451v1). A community writeup positioning it as a framework for automatic skeleton rigging of all 3D models — [ComfyUI Wiki, Apr 2025](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2025-04-12-unirig-one-model-to-rig-them-all); demo video — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zZWkQVxrdiw)
- **Rigify** is free, bundled with Blender, produces a humanoid rig with IK/FK and face controls from a manually placed metarig, but needs cleanup for game export including fixing the deform hierarchy — [CGDive](https://cgdive.com/rigify-vs-auto-rig-pro-auto-rigging-comparison/)
- **Auto-Rig Pro** at **$50 Full / $25 Lite** one-time provides automatic bone placement, the **Remap** retargeting tool, face rigs, and **Unity/Unreal/Godot export presets**; compatible through **Blender 5.1** — [3dxdev](https://3dxdev.com/auto-rig-pro-alternatives-in-2026-free-paid-blender-rigging-tools-compared/); [Superhive listing](https://superhivemarket.com/products/auto-rig-pro)
- Auto-Rig Pro's Remap "will retarget according to the bones names matches and bones original orientation"; **Blender's own constraints can also build custom retargeting** — [MocapOnline](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/skeleton-3d-model-rigging-games)
- The **SMPL Blender add-on** (Meshcapade) lets you edit, reshape and animate **SMPL-H, SMPL-X and SUPR** bodies, each with a mesh, a shape-specific skeleton, and blendshapes for body shape, facial expressions and pose correctives — [GitHub: Meshcapade/SMPL_blender_addon](https://github.com/Meshcapade/SMPL_blender_addon). Note it is subject to the SMPL licence terms in Q3.
- Working offline-capable reference pipelines for video → motion → rigged character: **prompt2rig** (fast video model + **GVHMR** + live Mixamo retargeting) — [GitHub](https://github.com/blendi-remade/prompt2rig); **Motion2MixamoRig** — [GitHub](https://github.com/MaxLiu22/Motion2MixamoRig)
- Open-source retargeting bone tables for **SMPL, VRM, Daz and UE5** exist as a concrete artifact to reuse — [autorig-workbench PR #76](https://github.com/sambena/autorig-workbench/pull/76)
- A 2026 guide roundup of Blender game-dev addons — [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/best-blender-addons-game-developers)
- Auto-Rig Pro's automatic bone placement quality **depends on clean mesh geometry** — [3dxdev](https://3dxdev.com/auto-rig-pro-alternatives-in-2026-free-paid-blender-rigging-tools-compared/)

### Inferences
- A viable 2026 offline stack, with each step sourced above: generate/clean the mesh in Blender → **UniRig** for the skeleton + skinning (or Rigify/ARP if you want a known-topology rig) → hand-fix pelvis pivot and shoulder weights (the two documented auto-rig weak points) → **Auto-Rig Pro Remap** or hand-built Blender constraints to retarget the AI/mocap source skeleton onto it → Auto-Rig Pro's Unreal/Unity export preset for FBX. Total paid cost: $50. Everything else is free or open-source.
- The offline stack's real limitation is **not rigging but validation**: UE's IK Retargeter gives you Auto Align, Snap Character to Ground, pose matching and an Op Stack with explicit root-motion handling (Q2), while the Blender path gives you name-and-orientation matching and whatever constraints you write. For a fighting game where frame-exact root motion matters, doing the retarget *inside* UE (as BEDLAM 2.0 does) and using Blender only for rigging is the better-sourced division of labour.
- Auto-Rig Pro's dependence on both **bone names and original bone orientation** means that an auto-rigged skeleton with non-standard names (the Meshy case) will fail Remap for the same reason it failed UE's fuzzy chain matching. Standardizing bone names immediately after auto-rigging — before any retargeting tool sees the rig — removes a whole class of failures in both pipelines.
